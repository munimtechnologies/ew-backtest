Generated 2026-10-29 14:49 UTC from public CDC data (NWSS WVAL by site, dataset atcp-73re; NSSP ED visit percentages, dataset rdmq-nq56), weeks 2022-01-01 to 2026-10-17. Wastewater data updated 2026-10-23; ED data build 2026-10-28.

The rule (fixed in advance, pre-registration): it fires when a series is above its own trailing 52-week median and has risen two weeks running. An alert is the first firing week after 4 quiet weeks. It runs on state wastewater (blue) and, as the baseline, on ED visits alone (orange). In the charts, dashed blue lines are wastewater alerts, dotted orange lines are ED-only alerts and wide grey bands are ED surge onsets (ED percent above its trailing 75th percentile for two weeks after 4 quiet weeks). Each line has its own scale. How often these alerts were right in the past is measured by the back-test in the ew-backtest repository.

Limits. State medians hide very different sewersheds, and few sites make a noisy median. For influenza A and RSV, most sites report a WVAL of exactly 1.0, their own baseline, for much of the year (58% of site-weeks in CDC's site file on 2026-10-23), so a state median often stays at 1.0 until more than half of a state's sites rise (site-file audit). A source whose newest week is more than 2 weeks older than the newest week on this page is marked stale and is not counted as firing. Both sources are revised after release and come out on different days. A firing rule is a signal to look closer, not a forecast. This is research information for public-health readers, not medical advice, not a forecast for any person or place, and not a CDC product (no endorsement implied).

How reliable have these alerts been?

From the pre-registered back-test on data fetched 2026-10-28, all states pooled. An ED surge is the ED percent rising above its trailing 75th percentile for two weeks after four quiet weeks. A surge is caught when an alert falls 0 to 4 weeks before its onset or in the onset week. An alert is right when a surge onset follows within 4 weeks; alerts during a surge already under way are left out of PPV. PPV by chance gives the same number of alerts random timing. Both rising means the wastewater rule firing in a week when the ED percent also rose; it is descriptive and was not graded. The ED-only rule shares its noise with the surge definition, which favours it on PPV and works against it on lead.
